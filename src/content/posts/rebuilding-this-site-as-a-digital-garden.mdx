A portfolio page is a snapshot. It says what you'd shipped on the day you last
edited it, and then it rots quietly. I wanted something that accumulates instead.

## What changed

The old site was one HTML file: hero, skills, projects, publications. It was fine.
It also hadn't meaningfully changed in years, because there was nowhere to put a
half-formed thought. Adding a section felt like a commitment.

Now there are two kinds of writing. Dated posts like this one, and
[notes](/notes) that carry a growth stage instead of a date and get revised
whenever I learn better. The rules are written up in [[How this garden works]].

## The interesting part is the link graph

Notes reference each other with `[[wiki-links]]`. Two build-time pieces make that
work:

1. A **remark plugin** that resolves each `[[target]]` against the set of entries
   that actually exist, and renders unresolved ones as visible "seeds" rather than
   dropping them.
2. A **graph module** that walks every entry's raw body, extracts its outgoing
   links, and inverts them into a backlink map.

Because both run at build time over files in the repo, the backlinks can't drift out
of sync with the content. There's no database to reconcile.

## Why files, not a hosted CMS

I write through a CMS admin UI, but it commits MDX to the repo rather than storing
rows in someone else's database. That keeps the link graph computable, keeps the
content greppable, and means the whole site is still just a folder I own.
